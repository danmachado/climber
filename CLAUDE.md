# Climber

A single-screen precision platformer for a wall-mounted Android display (the "frame"), plus a room builder. Served by GitHub Pages from the repo root on `main`.

## Layout

- `index.html`, `builder/index.html` are BUILT OUTPUT. Do not edit them by hand.
- `src/` is the source. Run `node src/build.js` to rebuild both pages.
  - `template.html` is the game, `builder-template.html` is the builder.
  - `shared-tiles.js` (tile art), `shared-hero.js` (hero sheet, generated by `make-hero.py`), `shared-config.js` (Supabase URL and public key) and `gen.js` (auto-builder) are injected into both pages.
- `tests/` are Playwright scripts. Run with `NODE_PATH=/opt/npm-tools/node_modules node tests/<name>.js`.
  - `gentest.js N` generates rooms across every setting and plays each with an auto-player. `gentest.js 0` checks the built-in starter rooms.
  - `maptest.js` checks the menu, map, stars and saved progress. `padtest.js` checks controller setup with a simulated pad. `mech.js` checks ice, crumble blocks and moving platforms. `talltest.js` checks tall rooms, springs, the gem and the builder height toggle. `moves.js` checks the wall moves (slide ramp, neutral jump, wall bounce, climb-jump refund, the mantle onto a one-tile post). `chain.js` checks room-to-room flow. `btest.js` and `pubtest.js` check the builder and publishing (against `mock.js`). `trials.js` checks the trial rooms (below); `trialshots.js` draws each one with its run on top, `trialtrace.js` prints a run frame by frame.
- `controller-test/` and `perf/` are diagnostic pages for the frame.

## Rules

- **Every level element must be in the auto-builder.** When a new element is added to the game, in the same change also add it to: the builder palette, `src/gen.js` (so auto-build can place it, behind a "Pieces to use" toggle), and the auto-player in `tests/gentest.js`. Then run `gentest.js` with a large N and get zero failures before shipping.
- Layouts: `generateRoom` dispatches on `opts.layout`: `floors` (default, stacked corridors with obstacles, `generateFloors`), `islands` (`generateIslands`) or `mixed` (by seed). The builder's Auto-build panel has a Layout select. About half the built-in levels per world are islands (`layout: 'islands'` in `build.js`).
- Island rooms: no corridors. The floor is island 0; the route climbs from slab to slab with moves recorded in the plan (`plan.kind === 'islands'`, one record per island with `move`): `hop` (a measured jump or jump+dash recipe: `REACH` lists, per rise and gap, where the feet land; `land` is how many tiles of the slab the landing may use), `climb` (a pillar flush with the slab edge, the landing joins its top), `chimney` (a two-step pillar), `spring` (six rows up, drift onto the slab), `zip` (a mover at the slab's end rides a track up, stops a row above a slab one tile over), `shelf` (the turn: stand `land` tiles in, jump straight up, drift onto a shelf over the slab's far part, leave the other way). A `need` grid keeps the route's air clear (standing room over every slab, the jump arc of every hop, pillar faces, the drift of a shelf); a piece is placed only where nothing is needed and its own needs are empty, so no slab ever sits in a jump. Landings stay five tiles short of the side walls so a shelf and the way back always fit; `generateIslands` retries a few seeds and keeps the first route that reaches the top. Hazards: on the floor under gaps (a miss costs a life), a spike or flame on the near lip of a low hop landing that the recipe clears (`lip`), spikes on the far part of a slab under a shelf (never walked), and the slab left behind by a hop into another hop in the same direction can turn to ice or crumble (`run`: the auto-player runs straight across without stopping). `tests/gentest.js` solves island plans with `solveIslands` (one recipe per move); `LAYOUT=islands node tests/gentest.js N` sweeps island rooms (the default sweep is floors).
- Auto-built rooms get a shaping pass in `src/gen.js` (stepped walls, hanging ceilings, backdrop tile `b`). It may only fill space the route does not need (see the `need` clearance array). `ONE='{"opts":{...},"seed":N}' node tests/gentest.js 1` traces a single room.
- Worlds and levels: `src/shared-worlds.js` lists the worlds and their themes. A room's data carries `world`, `level` and optional `par` (no database columns). Every slot (world + level number) holds its built-in room unless a published level has the same world and number, which takes over just that slot. The builder's Game map panel opens any slot for editing and publishes back to it (`tests/slottest.js`). A room's `level` is its permanent slot number, not its place on the map: the place comes from the map order, one extra published record named `(map order)` whose data is `{kind:'map', order:{world:[slot numbers in display order]}}`. The game and builder both skip it as a room. The builder hides slot numbers and shows positions. Progress and stars are saved on the device (`climber.progress.v1`). `#unlock` on the game URL opens everything for testing. `tests/maptest.js` covers menu, map, scoring and saving.
- Par times for built-in levels come from the auto-player: `gentest.js 0` writes `src/starter-times.json`, and `build.js` sets par to that time x 1.4. After changing `gen.js` or the starter sets, run build, then `gentest.js 0`, then build again.
- The idle demo loops random built-in levels by replaying the auto-player's recorded inputs (`src/starter-demos.json`, also written by `gentest.js 0`). The replay depends on physics being deterministic; after any physics or level change, re-record (build, `gentest.js 0`, build) and run `tests/demotest.js`.
- Stamina is one meter (`P.stamina` 110): climbing drains it, a dash costs `P.dashCost` (60, so one dash per full meter), standing on anything refills it, and the `o` pickup refills it in mid-air. Levels must be beatable without pickups. The hand-made first room needs the full 110 for its long climbs, so do not lower it without checking that room and Dan's published levels.
- Controllers: settings are saved per pad in `climber.pad.v2` as `layout` (which raw input is which physical button, learned by the setup screen when the browser does not report a standard pad) and `act` (what each button does, editable in Settings, Controls). Settings is a list of categories (`SET_PAGES` in template.html: `top` lists CONTROLS and GRAPHICS; `controls` is a small controller with a callout per button (its printed name from `padLetters`, Xbox or Nintendo/8BitDo lettering, and its job), then one row per action listing its buttons, then REDO SETUP, RESET BUTTONS, BACK; choose an action and press a button to give it that job, press one it already has to take it off, and every action keeps at least one button; `graphics` holds the PARTICLES, SHAKE, CITY LIFE and SPEED READOUT switches, label left, ON/OFF right). Back steps up one level (page to list, list to menu). A new setting goes into the page it belongs to, or a new page added to `SET_PAGES.top`. `tests/settingstest.js` covers the navigation. Inputs can come from any connected device or as key presses. Actions are one table, `ACTIONS` in template.html (with `KEY_ACT` for the keyboard key): a new action such as an attack is one more entry there, its flag in `readInput`, and its handling in `step()`; the Controls page, callouts and button lists pick it up from the table. `tests/controlstest.js` covers callouts, assigning and removing (run with `PAD='8BitDo Pro 2'` for the Nintendo lettering).
- Pieces are grouped by world: each entry in `WORLDS` lists the `tiles` and auto-builder `elements` that belong only to that world (ice is Icehouse only); everything else is common. The builder palette and the auto-build options show only the room's world, and `gen.js` never places another world's pieces. A new world's pieces go in its `WORLDS` entry. `tests/worldtest.js` covers this.
- Temple (world 4, theme `jungle`): the Death Fight Forever inspired world. Acid yellow stone with heavy mortar and glyph marks, vines as edge art, crates, hanging vines, pink flare torches and glyph blocks as decals, pink accent. The background is the inside of a ruined temple (Dan, Oct 2026), set by `THEMES.jungle.ruins` and painted in `drawSky` by `ruinsBack`/`ruinsFront` in shared-tiles.js, depths back to front: daylight cyan sky and cloud banks (no stars), two rows of hazy treetops, the far pyramid and palms (`skyline: 'pyramid'`, hazed `far` colours), a pale second wall with big holes and pale vines, then the room's own back wall (7 px courses, fluted pilasters, a carved fret frieze) with large lumpy holes (`holes`/`field`; whole blocks fall out inside a hole, rim blocks break raggedly, pilasters and the frieze are sturdier so columns and lintels often stand across a gap). Broken tops are lit with a cut face under them, undersides dark, and the sky under a broken course takes a 4 px cast shadow. Plants on the near wall: ivy patches near breaks, creepers down the face, moss, grass and the odd bush on broken tops, vines hanging from every broken underside, and vines draped across holes with strands. Value by depth: each nearer layer darker, the terrain brightest. Both walls are painted into pixel buffers (`put`), about 15 ms a screen headless (32 for a tall room), at room build only. Backdrop `b` tiles are `backKind: 'stone'` (dark ashlar piers, moss on top, vines under). Temple pieces: `L` is a laser emitter, a floor box that fires a three tile magenta beam on the jet cycle (same `jets` list, `laser: true`); `O` is a patrol drone that rides a `-` track like a fireball, pauses at each end and fires a three tile beam straight down only at its home end (`drones`, `stepDrones`, `droneBeam`). In `gen.js` a jet obstacle becomes a laser when `lasers` is on, and `drone` lays a four tile ceiling track with the drone's home at the far end so the auto-player runs under while it is away. `OWN` in gen.js lists every world-only piece. Levels are `SET4` in build.js plus the tall `The Pyramid`.
- Neon Row (world 5, theme `neon`): the night city. A lavender night sky with a `towers` skyline that fills the screen: three rows of separate buildings with streets between them (`cityRow` in `drawSky`, roof masts and tanks, a warm haze and a lamp post at the foot of each street). Colour rule (Oct 2026, after Katana ZERO city shots, patterns only): value by depth, the far haze row is pale lavender just under the sky, each nearer row a clear step darker, the terrain darkest, so the play layer always reads; each row keeps one hue (`CP` in drawSky, `THEMES.neon.city` can override: `haze`, `mid`, `near` with `body`, `hi`, `lo`, `pane`, window colours, `rimL`/`rimR`) and its detail is a tone step of it (a lit parapet, a ledge every four floors, a drainpipe, air-con boxes and balconies, a dark pane in every window slot); saturated colour only on signs, lit windows and the play layer; the near row has a faint cyan rim on its left edges and magenta on its right. Windows lit in clusters (a building picks its lit floors and lights run in groups along a floor), some sky signs hang vertically (`vert`, glyphs stacked), one pale beam tower, cloud bands, sagging overhead cables, still diagonal rain baked in, steel and concrete walls with panel seams and rivets, a lit cyan kerb strip along every top edge (`strip`/`stripLo` in `roughen`), a dark office-window backdrop (`backKind: 'windows'`), decals: air-con units, vending boxes, cable bundles, magenta tube lamps, vent grilles. Signs: `placeSigns` in `shared-tiles.js` hash-places small lit rectangles (a frame, a dim line, 2 to 5 glyphs of the made-up `GLYPHS` alphabet, four colours) on backdrop runs, inside wall masses and on the far towers (`SKY_SIGNS`, set by `drawSky`), baked lit into the static layer with a glow. Each sign has a kind: still, buzz (one glyph drops out for two ticks), pulse (a three second breath), scroll, cycle (two colours), and six far windows blink (`dot`). The game keeps only the animated ones in `signs` and `stepSigns` in `render` repaints a sign only when `signFrame` changes (one dirty rect, one chunk), so animation costs a few small blits; nothing strobes (no state change faster than 3 per second, dim never below 40 percent). Pieces: `w` is a spark on a cable (a fireball, `spark: true`, `drawSpark`) and `%` a holo sign (a jet with `holo: true`, `drawHoloBase`/`drawHoloWarn`/`drawHolo`); in `gen.js` the `wires` piece makes the fireball obstacle place `w` and `holos` makes the jet obstacle place `%`, so the auto-player's existing recipes cover both. Levels are `SET5` in build.js plus the tall `The Tower`. **City life** (game only, render only): for the towers skyline `drawSky` takes layer contexts `L` and paints the city in depths (`sky`, `far` rows, `farWin` middle-row windows, `near` row, `nearWin`, `cables`, then `front`: rain, terrain, decals; without `L`, as in the builder, it all goes into one canvas and looks the same) and leaves `CITY` (window slots per floor, cables, sizes). `newCity`/`cityStep` in template.html bring it to life inside small rectangles: runs of 2 to 4 office windows switch off or on every 1 to 3 s, the cables dip a pixel and lift every 3 to 4 s (`cableOff`, repainted per chunk column, one string carries bulbs), one flyer between the middle and near rows (`@mid`: a 7 x 3 car, or a third of the time a helicopter with a turning rotor and a one pixel bob) and one far car (head and tail light, `@far`, behind every row) at a time, on lanes at every height (the city's height is cut into bands and each band gets the row with the most open sky, so traffic passes high and low and slips behind buildings), up to two steam plumes (off a middle or near rooftop, `@mid`/`@near`, or up from the street between near buildings; four faint puffs, 3 updates a second), and an airship with a glyph ticker panel and a once a second nav light every minute or so. An occlusion map (`occ`: terrain, near row, far rows) keeps lanes in open sky and skips marking anything hidden. `cityCompose` paints the layers for one rectangle with the movers between them; `cityRepaint` does that into the static layer itself (movers are baked in) and copies the rectangle to the buffer, so a frame where nothing in the city changed costs nothing. The city runs on its own real-time clock (`ct`, 60ths of a second from `cityT`), not the frame count, so it keeps its pace on a slow screen. The occlusion map is read back through a throwaway canvas: reading a layer directly can move it off the GPU for good, which made every draw from it slow on the frame (Oct 2026: city life crawled there). The speed readout shows `CITY n`, city changes that reached the screen in the last second. Settings row CITY LIFE (`climber.city.v1`, on by default) turns it off. `__climber.city(on|'ship')` reads the stats and the running chunk copy count (`chunkCopies`); measured headless Oct 2026: a Neon room idles at about 155 to 185 chunk copies a second, city life adds about 40 to 90 (steam about 20 of that). Not yet seen on the frame: sign readability, sky banding, FPS with signs animating, FPS with city life on versus off.
- Furnace (world 3) pieces: `F` is a flame on the ground (a hazard like spikes, drawn animated each frame from the `flames` list) and `*` is a fireball that flies along `-` or `|` track tiles the way platforms do, but passes through everything and burns on touch (`findFireballs`, `stepFireballs`). `J` is a fire jet: a floor vent that fires a three tile column on a fixed cycle (`P.jetOff`/`jetWarn`/`jetOn`, offset per column, `jetPhase`), deadly only while it burns. `~` is lava: everything below the top lava tile, and after `P.lavaDelay` it rises at `P.lavaRise` px/s (`lava`), reset on respawn; the auto-builder puts it under the first floor of a tall Furnace room. In `gen.js`, floor spikes become flames in the Furnace, `fireball` is an obstacle the auto-player times a run under, and `jet` is one it waits out.
- Hero: `HERO_CHOICE` at the top of `src/make-hero.py` picks the character (`lantern` drawn in code, or a sprite pack folder such as `hero-mustache` or `hero-wolf`). To switch: change that line, run `python3 make-hero.py` in `src/`, then `node src/build.js`. A new pack needs a `ROLES` entry for its recolour and effect colours. The current hero is `hero-wolf`, the lone climber: its strips are generated by `src/hero-wolf/draw.py` from parts (edit the parts, run it, then make-hero and build). Its head is a thin band no wider than the face, hair tufts up and out the back, a nose pixel, no eyes. Every frame is posed with `pose()`: the hand-drawn head always sits on the torso (chin row on the row above it, so it can never float), the torso sits on the belt row, and arms and legs are lines between joints (arms via `Pose.arm`: always 4 px shoulder to hand, 5 when reaching (`ARM_LEN`/`ARM_REACH`, the joints give the direction and the arm is cut or extended to length), tapered and shaded, a two pixel upper arm lit on the front or top and shadowed on the back or underside, a one pixel forearm and a lit hand; the far arm uses `S` and `T`), layered far arm, far leg, torso, near leg, head, near arm (far limbs a shade darker, near leg lit `Q`), then one outline pass. Boots are tall and narrow (Celeste-like proportions, original pixels): two wide, the width of the leg, a sole plus a cuff over the bottom of the leg, and `Pose.boot` snaps each boot under the end of its leg (at most one pixel ahead or behind) so feet never stick out like a board. The run is eight side-on frames (contact, down, pass, up, then the other leg; legs hand-pixelled in `RUN_LEGS`, arms swing against them, the body bobs two rows), the idle breathes (chest rises with head and shoulders, then the knees give a pixel). An animation may play once and then loop from a frame (`loopFrom` in the manifest, `from` in `HERO.anims`, used by `fall`: apex, then a two frame flail), and the run's stride slows with the speed. **After any change run `python3 audit.py` in `src/hero-wolf`**: the sprite quality test (one piece per frame, no empty row or column, knot present and behind the face, boots on the ground row in grounded animations, head on the torso, idle body moves, run boots swap and arms swing against the legs, squash and stretch heights, hands on the wall face, no boot wider than 2 px, idle arms shaded and two pixels thick somewhere, every arm 4 or 5 px from the manifest's per-frame `arms`). It must PASS. `preview.py` writes `preview.gif`, every animation at its real speed. Its bandana tails are not in the sheet: the game simulates them as two five-node chains drawn as one-pixel lines about six pixels long (`stepChain`/`drawChain`, `chain.a`/`chain.b`) hung from the knot pixels, whose position per frame comes from the manifest (`anchors`), plus a short lock of hair (`chain.h`, colour `chain.hair`) off the back of the head that streams in a run. The chain colour is the dash state (`ROLES[...].chain`): red when a dash is available, grey when spent, white flash on refill, amber pulse when tired. `spentPairs` lets a pack recolour several shades when the dash is spent. Motion on top of the frames (all in `drawHeroState`, render only, physics untouched): squash and stretch as one scale about the feet (`squash`/`easeScale`; only ever a stretch of up to 1.5, never a shrink, because shrinking a 16 px sprite drops the one-pixel legs and the boots look detached: a jump stretches tall, a landing stretches wide, a dash stretches along its direction; the buffer draws sprites with smoothing off), idle one-shots `idle_look`/`idle_tie`/`idle_flex` after 3 s still (one loop in five, weights 5/3/1), and death as a ring burst in bandana red with the body fading to a silhouette (`drawDeath`). Also render only: `duck`, `look_up` and `skid` poses picked from the held input (`pl.inX`/`pl.inY`), and dust puffs (`puff`/`drawDust`) on hard landings, run starts, skids and wall slides.
- Trial rooms (`src/trials/*.json`, written by `node src/trials-make.js`): hand-made rooms built around one move each, not generated. Freefall (steer down a spiked drop between alternating spike plates, then wall-jump a chimney with spiked stretches), Cascade (exit at the bottom: fall through each hole onto a one-tile post set off to the side, jump and dash across the spikes to the next hole), Dash Only (one-tile posts too far or too high to jump, spiked walls, floor and ceiling), Pinpoint (posts under spiked ceilings three rows up: a full jump is death, every hop is a tap), Handhold (spike-topped walls, one-tile nubs to rest on, climb most of the meter away then climb-jump over), Kickback (a hyper under a low spiked ceiling, then a super across a pit). `build.js` appends them after each world's own levels, easier first, with `trial: true` and par = recorded time x 2; their recordings join the idle demo. A trial never holds the next world back (`worldOpen`) and a trial in the way never locks the level after it (`levelOpen`); the map frames trials in the accent colour and names them TRIAL. The builder keeps the flag. `tests/trials.js` replays each room's recorded run (`src/trial-runs.json`) in the real game; a missing or failing recording (or `SOLVE=1`) runs a beam search over inputs through the room's waypoints (`tests/trial-routes.json`) using the `snap`/`restore`/`tune` test hooks. `NEED=1` also searches with the room's move switched off (`needs` in trials-make: `dash`, `grab,bounce`, `super`; `+dash` means it must stay beatable without) and expects that to fail. The search and the replay treat standing with part of the hero over a spike tile as a fall (spike boxes are a pixel short of their tile; no person plays that). The wall bounce only gains about 25 px over jump + wall jump + dash in this physics, so no room can require it. None use the `o` pickup. Texture: trials-make draws each layout as clean boxes, then `texture()` makes it read as rock: the frame and thick masses grow lumpy outcrops (low-frequency noise), shelves and plates grow lumps underneath, outer corners of thick rock wear round and inner corners fill in (fillets), sealed hollows open inside thick rock onto the backdrop, and ragged backdrop panels cling to the rock. All of it stays two tiles clear of every tile the recorded run touches (`src/trial-keep.json`, written by `tests/trialkeep.js`), so the run plays frame for frame the same. A new top surface would be a new place to stand, so solid only grows bare where it hangs from rock or sits four tiles from the route outside a pit; anywhere else it is a one-tile nub crowned with spikes. Per room: `R.tex = { rag, clear, back, pockets, round, fillet, crown }`, or `R.tex = null` for clean boxes. After editing a trial: trials-make, `NEED=1 node tests/trials.js` (each search takes 1 to 5 minutes), and if a run changed, `tests/trialkeep.js` then trials-make again and replay; then build and `tests/demotest.js`.
- Wall moves follow Celeste's released Player.cs numbers (scaled by K): the wall slide starts slow and ramps to a full fall over `wallSlideTime`; a wall jump with no direction held is a neutral jump (no forced push); jumping during a straight-up dash beside a wall is a wall bounce (`bounceH`/`bounceV`, no extra stamina); a climb jump followed by pushing away within `wallBoostTime` refunds its stamina and becomes a wall jump; jumping during a sideways dash on the ground is a super (`superH`, full jump height) or, holding down, a hyper (`hyperH`, half height). `tests/moves.js` covers all of these. Topping out a wall (holding up as the hands clear the lip) is a mantle, not Celeste's climb hop (Dan, Oct 2026): the hero pops up (`climbHop`), slides over the lip at `mantleSpeed` and stops exactly one hero width plus a pixel past the face (centred on the edge tile, so it works on a one-tile post), held there until it touches down (`pl.mantle`, `mantleTime` cap); held direction is ignored meanwhile, and jump, dash or a spring cancel it. It never carries you a tile inward, so a spiked lip is not something to hop over: clear one with a climb jump (Handhold's run does).
- Rooms are one or two screens tall (48 or 96 rows). The game draws the whole room into a `world` layer of chunks and scrolls a tall room by sliding that layer with a transform (`applyCam`, snap on spawn), never by repainting. `RH` is the room height, `LH` the screen. The builder's Height select changes rows; `gen.js` takes `opts.rows`. Three tall built-in levels sit at slot 9 of each world. `tests/talltest.js` covers the camera, springs, the gem and the height toggle.
- Zip movers (`z`): painted like a platform on the same `-`/`|` tracks, but it waits where it is until you stand on it, then shoots to the far end (`P.zipSpeed`), rests, and crawls back (`zs` is its state: idle, go, rest, back). In `gen.js` a ferry or lift becomes a zip when the `zips` piece is on (`zipPick`); the auto-player treats an idle zip like a waiting platform.
- Keys and doors: `k` is a key that follows you once touched (like the gem) and is used up on the first closed door you reach; `D` is a locked door tile, solid until opened (`openDoor` opens every joined door tile). Both reset on respawn. The builder warns when a room has a door and no key. In `gen.js` the `keys` piece puts one key on the landing strip of a middle floor and a door in front of the exit.
- Springs (`u`) bounce you up at `P.springV` and refill the meter; the auto-builder uses them as a way up (`spring` connector, floor height fixed at 6, no walls on that floor). The gem (`G`) is optional: touch it and it follows you, it is banked when you next stand on solid ground (not ice), lost if you die first, and it is the fourth star of a level that has one.
- Play HUD: one top bar, no bottom bar (`drawTopBar`, `HB` gives its outline). A steel plate 17 px deep at the sides whose middle panel (`HB.cl` to `HB.cr`) drops to 28 px with chamfered corners, a pale edge along the outline and a neon line in the world accent under the middle (Katana ZERO HUD shots, patterns only). Kept deliberately sparse (Dan, Oct 2026): left is a bolt icon and the stamina capsule (ten cells, amber while a dash is affordable, red when not, a tick at `P.dashCost`); the middle is the clock, big (font size 3, 2.4 past ten minutes), white, red once past par; right is the stars you would earn if you finished now (clear, falls, par, and the gem in teal; a lost star goes to an outline, map levels only) and the level tag as a key cap at the edge. No room name, falls count or par bar. Side items sit in one band (y 3 to 14) so the space above and below matches. The bar fades to 35 percent while the hero is under it (`hudFaded`, one style change) and redraws only when its signature changes (about once a second).
- Pause: Start on a pad (or Esc) in a level opens the pause menu (`pause`, `pauseButtons`, `drawPause` in template.html): RESUME, RESTART, MAP, MAIN MENU, in the level clear panel's look. Nothing steps while it is up (clock, movers and hazards hold still); Start, Esc or dash resume, and the press that picks RESUME is not taken as a jump. On a level clear panel Start still goes to the map; the builder test, the demo and the embedded game never pause. `tests/pausetest.js` covers it.
- Rooms are data (level format 1: a grid of tile characters). The game never hard-codes a room. New tiles must stay backward compatible.
- Art, levels, names and sounds are original. Nothing copied from other games.
- Look: the static layer is built once per room (`drawTileLayer`): sky gradient and far silhouettes (`drawSky`), backdrop, walls on a transparent layer with the roughen pass and per-world edge art (`drawSolid`/`roughen`), hash-placed decals (`drawDecals`) and baked glows (`drawGlow`) under flames, the exit and lamps. All of it lives in `shared-tiles.js` so the builder matches. Particles (`drawWeather`) are the only per-frame effect: their own screen-sized canvas, off by default, toggled in Settings (`climber.fx.v1`).
- The frame is slow at repainting its 4K screen. The game draws to a 270 x 480 buffer and only copies changed pieces to the screen. Mark dirty rectangles for anything that moves. No full-screen redraws per frame, no flashing or strobing. The HUD canvas is full size but in play only its two strips are cleared and copied. The frame's speed lab (`perf/`, Oct 2026: empty loop 49 fps, one moving sprite 37 to 39, transforms slower than left/top) says mid 30s is its ceiling; shake and particles are Settings toggles (`climber.shake.v1`, `climber.fx.v1`) so their cost can be checked there.
